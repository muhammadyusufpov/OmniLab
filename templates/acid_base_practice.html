Method

Find the proton transfer before you balance.

Identify the acid and the base. Ask what accepts a proton, then predict the products. Count atoms on each side and change coefficients, never subscripts. Finally, check whether water forms or whether another product explains the reaction.

Try each question before you open its answer. The first two are familiar neutralizations. The later questions include ammonia, bicarbonate, and a small amount comparison. This set is about equations and reasoning. It does not calculate pH or replace a lab procedure.

Work through the reactions

Six acid-base problems

Each answer shows the step that matters. Open a linked guide when you want more context.

01Neutralization

What forms when hydrochloric acid meets sodium hydroxide?

Write the molecular equation. Then remove ions that do not change to write the net ionic equation.

Show the worked answer

HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l)

Hydrogen ions and hydroxide ions form water. Sodium and chloride remain dissolved. Each formula unit already balances with coefficient 1. Remove the spectator ions to get H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l).

A balanced equation alone cannot tell you the final pH. The amounts and concentrations matter.

02Two acidic hydrogens

How much potassium hydroxide balances sulfuric acid?

Start with H₂SO₄ + KOH → K₂SO₄ + H₂O. Find the potassium coefficient before you balance water.

Show the worked answer

H₂SO₄(aq) + 2KOH(aq) → K₂SO₄(aq) + 2H₂O(l)

Potassium sulfate has two potassium atoms, so put 2 before KOH. Those two hydroxide groups combine with the two acidic hydrogens to form two water molecules. Check oxygen last: six on each side.

The 1:2 ratio counts formula units in this equation. It does not set a safe mixing amount for a physical experiment.

03No water product

Does every acid-base reaction make water?

Predict what forms when ammonia accepts a proton from nitric acid. Look for a hydroxide group before you add water.

Show the worked answer

NH₃(aq) + HNO₃(aq) → NH₄NO₃(aq)

Ammonia accepts a proton and becomes ammonium. Nitrate is the accompanying ion. No hydroxide group appears on the reactant side, so adding water would invent atoms. One of each reactant balances the equation.

Acid-base describes proton transfer. The salt-and-water shortcut fits many acid-plus-hydroxide problems, not every acid-base pair.

04Gas formation

What products follow when acetic acid meets sodium bicarbonate?

Write a balanced equation. Explain why the products include a gas as well as water and a salt.

Show the worked answer

CH₃COOH(aq) + NaHCO₃(aq) → CH₃COONa(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)

Bicarbonate accepts a proton, forming carbonic acid. Carbonic acid can break down into water and carbon dioxide. The remaining acetate pairs with sodium. Every formula has coefficient 1 in the overall balanced equation.

Bubbles are an expected cue, not proof of a gas identity in an unknown real sample.

05Amount comparison

What remains after 0.10 mol HCl reacts with 0.08 mol NaOH?

Use the equation in question 1. Assume both reactants can mix fully, then compare their mole amounts.

Show the worked answer

0.10 mol HCl − 0.08 mol NaOH = 0.02 mol HCl remaining

The reaction uses one HCl for each NaOH. All 0.08 mol NaOH can react with 0.08 mol HCl, leaving 0.02 mol HCl. The mixture also contains 0.08 mol NaCl in this ideal amount calculation.

Do not infer a pH from these moles alone. A concentration requires the final volume, and real behavior can depend on conditions. OmniLab's reaction result does not model these amounts.

06Carbonate and acid

Why does sodium carbonate need two hydrochloric acid units?

Predict the gas, salt, and water. Check sodium and chlorine before hydrogen and oxygen.

Show the worked answer

Na₂CO₃(aq) + 2HCl(aq) → 2NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)

Two sodium atoms require two NaCl units. That calls for two chlorine atoms, supplied by 2HCl. The remaining two hydrogen atoms make one water molecule. Carbonate supplies the carbon for carbon dioxide.

This predicts a supported reaction pair. Fizzing alone does not identify a substance, and the model does not verify a physical sample.
