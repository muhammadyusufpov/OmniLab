How to use the lab

From a question to a reaction prediction

Start with a marked pair. The prediction appears only after you select Analyze Chemical Reaction.

  1. 1

    Choose your first chemical

    Open the Chemicals menu and select one of the 38 available substances. The lab then marks every chemical that forms one of its 34 supported pairs with your first choice.

  2. 2

    Add a supported partner

    Select the marked partner by click, tap, keyboard, or drag. You can also choose a flask, test tube, beaker, or burner to arrange the visual workspace.

  3. 3

    Analyze, then check the result

    Select Analyze Chemical Reaction. Read the equation, explanation, safety notes, and effect together, then compare important details with your course materials or instructor.

Read beyond the animation

Use the cue to ask a chemistry question

First, find AgCl(s) on the product side. The (s) means solid silver chloride. Then compare that solid with the white precipitate cue in the vessel.

Silver and chloride ions form that solid. Sodium and nitrate ions remain dissolved. This links the visible cue to the balanced equation.

The cue shows an expected observation, not a measured outcome. It cannot tell you the concentration, temperature, reaction rate, or yield in a physical experiment.

Choose the right tool

What this simulator can and cannot show

No single online lab covers every learning goal. Choose the format that matches what you need to practice.

Use OmniLab for a quick supported-pair check

Choose two chemicals and get a deterministic educational result without an account. This is the best fit when you want to practice reading an equation, explanation, safety notes, and observation together.

Use an open-ended lab for experimental design

A broader virtual environment is a better fit when you need hundreds of reagents, quantitative glassware, concentrations, measurements, or a multi-step procedure. OmniLab does not currently model those variables.

Use your course platform for assigned practicals

A teacher-selected simulation is the better fit when your grade depends on a specific protocol, dataset, assessment, or learning-management workflow. Follow your course requirements first.
