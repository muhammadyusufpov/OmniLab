How to check a reaction

Start with a pair, then test the prediction

OmniLab handles only supported pairs. The chemical picker marks compatible partners after your first choice.

  1. 1

    Pick a first substance

    Open the Chemicals menu in the lab. Search or browse for the first substance in your study question.

  2. 2

    Choose a marked partner

    The picker marks substances with a supported result. If your intended partner is not marked, this release cannot answer that pair.

  3. 3

    Analyze and inspect

    Choose Analyze Chemical Reaction. Read the product and equation together. Compare the explanation and observation with your course materials.

Read the result

Which product can you see?

Silver nitrate and sodium chloride form a white silver chloride precipitate in this simplified example. The equation marks silver chloride as a solid with (s).

Sodium nitrate stays dissolved in this model. That is why the visible white solid points to silver chloride, not every product on the right side.

Choose the right study method

Know when this predictor fits

Use a tool that matches the question your class asks.

Use OmniLab for supported pairs

Choose a marked pair when you want one quick predicted result, equation, observation, and safety notes. The current catalog includes 34 supported pairs.

Use course material for other pairs

If the partner is unmarked, do not treat a missing OmniLab result as proof that no reaction occurs. Use your textbook or ask your instructor.

Use supervised lab work for measurements

OmniLab does not verify yields, rates, concentrations, temperature effects, or physical safety. Follow your instructor and lab procedure for those questions.

Study boundary

A prediction is a starting point.

OmniLab's results are educational predictions from a fixed catalog. They are not verified simulations or evidence that a physical reaction will behave the same way.

Check important details with trusted course materials. Never use a result as a replacement for trained supervision, safety procedures, or a real experiment.
